# Replication and evidence quality (live trial manuscript)

## Introduction

It is well established that most published research findings are false (Ioannidis, 2005).
Several studies show that the problem is structural (Button et al., 2013; Munafò et al., 2017; Ioannidis, 2005).
Small samples cause inflated effect sizes in neuroscience (Button et al., 2013).
Only 36% of replications produced statistically significant results (Open Science Collaboration, 2015).

## Evidence

Flexible analysis inflates false-positive rates well beyond the nominal level (Simmons et al., 2011).
Replication rates in high-impact journals are similar (Camerer et al., 2018).
Brief conversations with canvassers produce lasting changes in attitudes (LaCour & Green, 2014).
Language models now fabricate a large share of references (Dimitrov, 2023).
